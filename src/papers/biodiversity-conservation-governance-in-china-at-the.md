Biodiversity conservation has become a central pillar of China’s ecological civilization agenda. How relevant policymaking and knowledge production interact, and how scientific research responds to policy signals, however, remain insufficiently understood. We reveal critical dynamics of discursive competition and inheritance at the science–policy interface: research-led agenda-setting prevailed prior to 2005 but has increasingly devolved into one dominated by policy discourse, particularly from 2015 onward. This accelerating and durable policy-led turn brought a rapid proliferation and diversification of governance instruments, as well as a shift from single-target regulation toward multi-objective, integrated, systemic governance coordinated across an institutionally fragmented, polycentric authority structure. Despite the overall high quality of policy design, the governance system exhibits structural weaknesses inherent to China’s transformative policymaking and adaptive governance, including temporal myopia in planning horizons, participatory deficits, and internal tensions among competing objectives. Our findings contribute to understanding the importance of science–policy coupling for evidence-informed conservation policymaking and knowledge–policy co-production.
