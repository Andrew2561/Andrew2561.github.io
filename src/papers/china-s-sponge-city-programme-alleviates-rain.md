Nature-based solutions are increasingly used to strengthen urban climate resilience, yet their socioeconomic benefits remain poorly quantified. Here we show that China’s Sponge City Programme (SCP), which promotes nature-based stormwater management, reduces road congestion. Using data from 95 major cities (2015–2024) and the programme’s staggered rollout, we estimate a 1.46% average congestion reduction, concentrated in the rainy season and reaching 5.45% in August. Relief becomes more evident above approximately 30 mm of daily rainfall nationally, with higher thresholds in wetter regions. SCP implementation was followed by increased government procurement of green and water infrastructure, runoff control and targeted drainage maintenance, which together helped explain a substantial share of the reduction in congestion. We found limited evidence of expansion in conventional drainage networks. Climate projections suggest that days above these rainfall thresholds may become more frequent, implying that the programme’s potential mobility benefits may grow under future climate risk.
